# Campfire Jam: what makes rhythm games fun (research notes, 10 lines)

1. Feel first: every press needs instant audio + visual confirmation (hit flash, ring, sparks) within one frame; input lag kills the genre, so calibration matters.
2. Combos are the core risk/reward loop: a growing streak makes each next note feel more valuable; milestone call-outs (10, 25, 50, 100) turn a number into a moment.
3. A visible, readable combo (big, centred, near the action) beats a small HUD label; players watch the lanes, not the corner.
4. Breaking a long combo should be noticed (a neutral "combo ended at 23"), not punished with shame; it fuels "one more try".
5. Near-miss feedback converts losses into goals: "1% more accuracy for the third star" is far more motivating than a bare 89%.
6. Personal bests are the healthiest chase: show the gain over the old best ("+340") and celebrate a new one clearly.
7. Restart friction must be near zero: a one-tap Play again at the top of the summary, plus a keyboard shortcut (R).
8. The first 30 seconds should be a guaranteed win: one obvious "Play" button that picks the easiest chart you have not mastered yet.
9. Short rounds (about a minute) plus a clear next goal (star, rank S, full combo, next tier) give the "just one more" pull without timers or streak guilt.
10. Juice must respect comfort: shake, sparks and pops scale down or turn off with reduced motion and low-graphics prefs; no fake urgency, no paid randomness.
