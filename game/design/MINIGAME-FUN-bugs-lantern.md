# Mini game fun notes: Bug Safari and the Lantern

1. Game feel first: every input needs an instant answer (swing arc, sound, hit pause or shake, score pop). A miss must also answer, or it feels broken.
2. Near-miss feedback ("So close!") turns a failure into a lesson and is the strongest "one more try" pull, as long as it is honest (only shown when a bug really was just out of reach).
3. Combos reward rhythm and planning: show the window draining, announce milestones (x3, x5) loudly, and say clearly when a chain breaks.
4. Short rounds (90 s) with a one-tap Play again (and a key) keep restarts under a second; the summary must lead with the result and the button, not with text walls.
5. Personal-best chase beats leaderboards for solo play: show the best on the HUD during the run and "N points to beat your best" or "new best by N" at the end.
6. Collection goals (journal, silhouettes, per-arena goals) give long-term reasons to replay a short round; each run should advance at least one visible bar.
7. Risk/reward: sneaking is slow but safe, walking is fast but scares bugs; rare spawns (gold beetle at 30 s) give a mid-run spike of tension.
8. Escalation: the last 10 seconds should feel different (timer colour/pulse, a soft warning tick) without guilt or fake urgency outside the round.
9. Variety between runs comes from seeded layouts (MMRng); no paid randomness, no daily streaks, nothing that expires.
10. Clarity in hubs (the Lantern): two numbers called "level" confuse players; name each one for where it lives (Lantern level vs Realm level) and keep targets 44 px+ and text 12 px+.
