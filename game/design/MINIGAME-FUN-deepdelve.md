# Deepdelve: what makes a dig game fun (research notes)

1. Short, legible loops win: a "dive" (surface -> deep -> back) gives a natural 2-5 minute round with a clear start and end (Downwell, SteamWorld Dig, Spelunky).
2. Risk/reward depth push: the deeper you go the more you earn, but you must get back out; surfacing alive multiplies the score (push-your-luck, like Dome Keeper's return trips).
3. Game feel ("juice"): every break needs instant feedback - sound, a small camera kick, a floating "+8 iron" - scaled down or off for reduce-motion and low-graphics players.
4. Combos reward rhythm and skill: chaining breaks inside a short window builds a visible multiplier that you can learn to keep alive.
5. Personal bests are the healthiest "one more try" hook: compare only with your own past self, never with timers, streaks or paywalls.
6. Near-miss feedback ("only 2 blocks short of your deepest dive") turns a loss into a concrete next target.
7. The end-of-run summary must be clear (score, depth, ores, combo, which were new bests) with one big "Dive again" button - zero friction restarts.
8. Escalating difficulty comes from depth itself: harder ores need better picks, lava, crawlers and the Rock Wyrm guard the deep layers.
9. Variety between runs comes from seeded worlds (?seed=N) and seeded ore/creature placement, so each seed is a different, fair puzzle.
10. Respect the player: no fake urgency, no loot boxes, no daily streak guilt; medals and bests only flow into the hub through the existing additive keys.
