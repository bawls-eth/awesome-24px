# Research brief: Realm of the Mad God (RotMG)

Sources: RealmEye wiki (loot containers, getting-started, game-objectives, nexus), RotMG Fandom wiki (Fame), Wikipedia, MMOBomb / PC Gamer / MMOs reviews, Steam reviews, RealmEye forum threads. Direct page fetches of RealmEye failed, so bag details come from search excerpts and general public knowledge. Treat exact numbers as approximate.

## 1. Core loop and pacing
- Persistent shared "Realm" server (about 85 players) with a hostile map. Players kill monsters for XP, level to 20, then hunt dungeons and bosses for gear. Each character is one short-to-medium run (tens of minutes to hours).
- Difficulty ramps by distance from the spawn Nexus. Beach is easy, then shore, midlands, highlands and godlands. Gear tier gates how far out you can survive.
- Realm Events: timed world bosses (for example Lost Sentry, Avatar, Hermit God). Each is announced on the map and in chat. Players converge on it and kill it together. Clearing enough "Realm Event" kills (the god quota) opens the Realm to Oryx's Castle.
- Dungeons are 5-20 minute instanced runs entered through portals dropped by bosses. Each has set room themes and ends in a boss. Cheap dungeons are one-shot practice, while harder ones (Cultist, Shatters, Void) are harder and drop the best gear.
- Boss design: readable telegraphs. Bosses use radial rings, aimed shots, spiral patterns and phase changes at HP thresholds. Many have a visible warning (a glow or text line) before a big attack. Skill, positioning and dodging matter more than raw stats.

## 2. Loot
- Item tiers T0-T14 for weapons and armor, T0-T6 for rings, with UT (untiered) items as special drops, many with unique effects. Higher tier means a higher base stat.
- Bag colours as a rarity signal: brown (potions, public), pink (low-tier gear, public), then soulbound bags: purple, blue (stat potions), cyan (high-tier), white (UT), orange/golden (blueprints, keys, tokens, skins). A red/boosted bag appears under loot-boost effects. Only the player the bag dropped for sees soulbound bags.
- Drop rule: soulbound drops depend on damage dealt as a share of the boss's HP. Contribute enough and you get your own roll. Public (brown/pink) bags can be taken by anyone.
- Trading: item trade between players is allowed in Nexus (with a confirm screen). Soulbound drops cannot be traded, but unbound ones can. This creates a market economy and some scam risk.
- Stat-boost items: stat potions (Life, Mana, Attack, Defense, Speed, Dexterity, Vitality, Wisdom) permanently raise the character's stats. Maxing all eight (the "8/8") is the endgame goal. The key feature is that this progress is lost on death.
- No in-game combining of items. Fusing applies to pets (Fame can be used for feeding or fusing pets).

## 3. Shop and currency
- Two currencies: Fame (earned on death, spent on small perks) and Realm Gold (bought with real money). Gold buys character slots, vault slots, keys for dungeons, skins, pet upgrades and consumables.
- Criticism: reviewers and players say it is "pay-to-win lite". There is no PvP, so it mostly affects progression speed. Complaints cover paywalled character slots, keys that skip dungeon grinding, selling pets that raise stats, and a long, repetitive grind for max stats and top loot. Others argue skill still dominates, because a paying player still dies to bad dodging.
- Ownership history: Kabam sold the game to DECA in 2016. Monetization pressure is a recurring theme in community feedback.

## 4. Permadeath
- Death is permanent. The character, its gear and its stat boosts are lost. New characters start at level 1.
- Persists: the Vault (shared storage chests that survive death), account Fame (earned when a character dies, scaled by deeds such as level, kills, and bosses killed), the Graveyard (record of dead characters), unlocked classes and skins, and pets.
- Retention: dying is a story event (graveyard entry, Fame payout) so loss feels like closure. Vault items let a death hurt without erasing everything. Seeing other players' graves keeps ambition alive. Each class unlocks via play (class quests), giving long-term goals.

## 5. Co-op and social
- All damage on a boss counts, so players do not steal kills. Soulbound loot means no loot-fighting and no fear of ninja-looting. Big events rely on crowds.
- Helping players matters: Priest and Paladin buffs, shared aggro, and healing. Chat tags and guilds are social anchors. The Nexus acts as a social hub for trades and portal runs.
- Public bags (potions, low items) are shared freely, reinforcing a generous feel.

## 6. Map and variety
- Realm maps are procedurally generated per server (biomes, terrain, structures, monster spawn zones) and reset periodically when Oryx is beaten. Dungeons are hand-built layouts with some randomness in room and monster placement.
- Fresh content comes from seasonal events, new dungeons and bosses, and balance reworks. Updates add new gear and reshuffle the meta.

## What Pixeltown's Mad Maze/Realm should borrow (free-to-play, no real-money purchases, no loot boxes)
1. Telegraph every boss attack (a 0.5s glow or ring warning) so deaths feel fair and learnable.
2. Per-player (soulbound-style) loot rolls that scale with damage dealt, so co-op never causes loot fights.
3. A "graveyard" screen after death showing wave reached, kills and a shard payout, which makes loss feel like closure.
4. A death cache that keeps a percentage of shards, increased by deeds such as boss kills (Fame-style banking).
5. A rarity colour cue on drops (bag/shard-glow colour per rarity) readable at a glance.
6. A timed mini-boss event every N waves with an on-screen announcement and a bonus drop for contribution.
7. Distance/wave-based loot tiers: later waves raise the drop tier, so the ramp itself is the loot gate.
8. Free cosmetic unlocks (skins or trails) earned from milestones, such as a wave count or a boss first-kill.

## Avoid
1. Paywalled slots, keys or stat boosts. Even "lite" pay-to-win drew heavy criticism.
2. Grindy, repetitive stat-maxing as the only endgame, which reviewers called repetitive.
3. Losing everything on death with no meta progress. Keep the death cache and some persistent unlocks.
4. Open item trading, which invites scams and market pressure. Keep exchange limited to the 3-to-1 combine.
