# New mini games 2: what makes them fun (Stack Tower, Echo Garden)

1. Timing-stack games live on one honest rule: the part of a block that misses falls off. The tower narrows, so every drop matters and the player always knows why a run ended.
2. A "perfect" window (a few pixels) that snaps the block flush, chains combos and, from 3 in a row, widens the block back. Skill earns room; no luck needed.
3. The swing starts slow and speeds up every few floors, and later floors swing from both sides. That is the skill curve; the first 30 s are slow enough for anyone.
4. Near-miss text ("2 px from perfect!", "1 floor to your best") turns a loss into a clear next try, without guilt or timers outside the round.
5. Memory-sequence games (Echo Garden) feel calm when the tempo is gentle, every flower has its own colour, shape and pitch (never colour alone), and a miss replays the pattern instead of ending the game.
6. Forgiving: Echo Garden gives three wilts per round; a miss costs one and shows the right flower. Stack Tower has no lives, it is the tower itself that shrinks.
7. Both games use seeded rounds (MMRng): "Replay seed" repeats the same swing order or melody, so a player can practise and improve.
8. A best marker where it is natural: Stack Tower draws a "best" line at your record floor; Echo Garden shows your best pattern length on the HUD.
9. Juice stays low-gfx-safe: a few chunky particles, a short pop scale, a soft tone. Reduced motion removes shake, falling-piece spin and blinks; Low graphics caps particles.
10. No dark patterns: no money, no streaks, no daily gates, no fake urgency. Medals are simple skill goals, sent to the Hub via `pixeltown-medals-<game>-v1`.
